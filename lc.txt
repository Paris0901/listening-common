Opening

The Listening Commons is an independent editorial space for conversations that resist haste, certainty and easy conclusions.

At a time when public discourse rewards reaction over reflection, The Listening Commons makes room for attention. Through long-form conversations, essays and interdisciplinary inquiry, it explores psychiatry, culture, consciousness, sexuality, science, art, human rights and the forces shaping the human condition.

Founded and created by Scott Douglas Jacobsen and co-created with Dr. Aninda Sidhana, the platform brings independent journalism into dialogue with psychiatric and lived insight. It is not a clinic, a conventional interview archive or another arena for competing voices. It is a place where difficult questions can remain open long enough for deeper understanding to emerge.

Founding vision

Listening is not the pause before speaking. It is a form of knowledge, dignity and relationship.

The Listening Commons was created to recover listening as a serious cultural practice—to encounter another person without immediately reducing their experience to a label, argument or conclusion.

Its purpose is to connect disciplines, cultures and forms of knowledge while preserving complexity, difference and human dignity.
